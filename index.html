A self-directed art program

“A billion wrongs in a drawing make it right.”

Art paired with positive, motivational conversation. No experience needed, and nobody has to be good at drawing. It gives people a way to say something they don’t have words for, and a reason to sit at a table with other people.

Kirk Charlton built it inside Eastern Oregon Correctional Institution while serving his own sentence. It has since run in recovery centers, care homes and schools.

The method

You don’t erase your mistakes. You learn to read them.

A sequence, on purpose. Every lesson runs the same three beats.

1

Make real art

A pencil, a page, and a prompt anyone can start. Nothing here needs talent and nothing here gets graded.

2

Connect life to art

The drawing becomes the thing to point at. It is easier to talk about a page than to talk about yourself cold.

3

Talk it through

Positive, motivational conversation around the table. The art gives the conversation somewhere to start.

Kirk runs it the same way he built it.

An easel, a marker, and whoever is in the room. The method did not come out of a curriculum binder, it came out of doing it for years in a place where nobody had materials.

At a glance

One method, four places it ends up.

The same twelve lessons, whether it is a wall, an easel, a book somebody works through alone, or a story read to a class.

Kirk Charlton

I’m not a success story. I’m a working artist.

Kirk spent more than twenty years incarcerated, and built Art Inside Out inside Eastern Oregon Correctional Institution during his final stretch of about eight years. He has spent the years since running the program, painting, and writing and illustrating children’s books.
